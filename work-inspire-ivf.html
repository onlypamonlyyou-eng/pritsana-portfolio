The challenge

A fertility clinic's website has to earn trust before it earns a click.

IVF, ICSI and PGT are not casual searches people arrive anxious, comparing clinics, and often reading in a second language. The site has to explain complex medical procedures clearly, keep pricing and success-rate claims accurate and defensible, and handle genuinely sensitive ground like surrogacy law without ever feeling clinical or cold.

That's a structure problem before it's a content problem: which service gets its own page, how deep the medical detail goes before a patient needs a human, and how the whole thing still has to perform in search against every other fertility clinic doing the same.

WHAT THE SITE HAS TO DO
  • Explain IVF / ICSI / PGT clearly, for a non-medical reader
  • Keep pricing and outcome claims accurate and sourced
  • Handle sensitive topics cost, success rates, surrogacy with care
  • Convert quiet research into a booked consultation
  • Rank against every other fertility clinic in the market

My approach

Structure the site the way a patient actually thinks.

Map the patient journey
Build service-page architecture
Ground content & pricing
Technical SEO & page speed
Hand off to LUNA where it fits

What I own

Website and SEO, end to end.

Information architecture

Service pages for IVF, ICSI and PGT structured around how a prospective patient actually searches and compares not around how the clinic is organised internally.

Technical & on-page SEO

Schema markup, header hierarchy, internal linking and Core Web Vitals the same technical foundation used across every site in this portfolio, applied to a healthcare context.

Content accuracy

Working with the clinical team to keep procedure descriptions, pricing and outcome language accurate sensitive-topic content doesn't get written on assumption.

Where LUNA fits in

The website handles research; LUNA handles the live conversation. I own both, so the handoff between "reading the ICSI page" and "chatting with LUNA about it" is one continuous experience, not two disconnected tools.
